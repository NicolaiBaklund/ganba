Three directions for Ganba

Each one takes a different piece of Japanese running culture and turns it into the app's look. Same data, same features. Every direction also applies the layout fixes listed at the bottom.

Phones scroll. Pick the one that feels most like you, or mix: "A's bib with B's training map" is a fine answer.

Today: week strip and rest daysNew

The week strip now shows your training. Each day gets a small sash stripe in the session's colour: solid when done, striped when planned. Two sessions on one day get two stripes. A white box still means you logged food that day. On rest days the bib shrinks to a small one that says what comes next.

Tasuki training mapNew

B's route map, drawn the Tasuki way. The line is a thick black track. Weeks are square markers, easy weeks get a dashed track, this week is a crimson block and race day is a chequered finish. The whole plan fits on one screen, and Adjust plan stays in the header.

Left
Pure map. Calmest, km as numbers only.
Right
Map plus a km bar for each week, so you also see the plan build up toward the peak.
Tap
Tapping a week opens its sessions under the marker, like this week is open here.

A. Tasuki

Ekiden relay energy. Today's session is a race bib you pin on, with a sash stripe in the session's colour. Heavy condensed numbers like bib print. Black, white and one crimson. Most "ganbaru" of the three: effort, grit, race day every day.

Type
Archivo, one family. Squeezed to 62% width for big numbers, normal width for text.
Signature
The bib on Today, the volume bar chart on Training.
Risk
Loud. Could feel intense on rest days.

B. Line

Tokyo transit map. Your plan is a train line running to race day, each week a station. Each session type gets a line colour and a round badge, like Ginza or Marunouchi. Today's session shows its structure as a little route. Friendly, clear, very readable.

Type
M PLUS 1, a Japanese signage-style sans with matching Latin letters.
Signature
The route map on Training, the station strip for the week.
Risk
Many colours. Needs discipline so it doesn't turn into a rainbow.

C. Ensō

Sumi ink and hanko seals. The calorie ring is an ensō, the Zen circle drawn in one brush stroke, inked as you eat. Logged days get a red seal. The plan reads as ink strokes, one per week. Calm and quiet: steady effort rather than race-day noise.

Type
Zen Maru Gothic, a soft rounded Japanese sans.
Signature
The ensō on Today, red seals for logged days.
Risk
Calm can read as spa or meditation app rather than training.

Layout fixes in every direction

  1. Today is home, not a sub-page.The back arrow and centered title go. A week strip at the top shows which days you logged; tap a day to look back.
  2. Session first, fuel inside it.One block tells you what to run and what to eat around it.
  3. Calories and macros in one compact block.The "Base, Activity, Planned run, Goal" line moves behind a tap on the number.
  4. Meals as one list.No card per meal. Three meals take a third of the height they do now.
  5. Adjust plan sits in the Training header.Whole plan visible at a glance; tap a week to open it. End plan moves into a menu.
  6. Fewer containers.Type size and weight carry hierarchy. Cards only where something is tappable as a unit.
  7. Copy pass.Helper notes removed unless they stop a mistake. No em dashes, no dot-separated chains.
